## Competition brief

I developed the $100 Combat Robot for Texas Roborumble at The University of Texas at Austin. The robot had to fit within the competition’s 3-pound weight limit while withstanding impacts and delivering effective hits. The weight limit made the chassis, drive system, and spinner part of the same design tradeoff.

## Chassis and fabrication

I used Onshape to model the chassis and lay out the mechanical components. I 3D-printed the chassis in thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) at 80% infill. The flexible material allowed the chassis to deform under impact, while the infill provided internal support without requiring a solid print.

## Drive and spinner

Two powered wheels provided the robot’s primary drive and steering. The robot used a vertical spinner with a hex bolt at each end, adding mass to the rotating assembly for competition impacts. I also observed that vibration from the spinning assembly could contribute to the robot’s movement during a match.

## Competition result

The robot finished Texas Roborumble with a **3–2–0 record**, earning three wins and two losses across five matches. Competing gave me practical experience with how a 3D-printed chassis and a two-wheel drive system behaved under repeated impacts.
